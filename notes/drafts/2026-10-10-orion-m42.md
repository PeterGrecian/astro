## What the frame shows

Orion in a single greyscale frame. Betelgeuse is the bright star at upper
left (1), Bellatrix at upper right (2), the belt in the middle (3), and the
sword with M42 near the bottom. A satellite or aircraft trail crosses the
sword.

## Betelgeuse's colour

It shows, even in one frame. Measured from the raw Bayer photosites (box
aperture over each 60 s trail, per-channel sky subtracted), with Rigel, a
near-white B8 star in the same frame, as the white reference:

| star | B/G relative to Rigel |
|---|---|
| Rigel | 1.00 |
| Alnitak | 0.77 |
| Alnilam | 1.00 |
| Mintaka | 1.17 |
| Bellatrix | 0.89 |
| Saiph | 0.87 |
| **Betelgeuse** | **0.21** |

The hot blue stars scatter by about +/-0.2 mag around Rigel (Peter probed the belt: three stars of nearly one colour give 0.77, 1.00, 1.17, which is the one-frame noise); Betelgeuse has a fifth
of their blue for its green, **1.7 mag redder**. The catalogue B-V difference
between the two is 1.88, and the camera's green is broader than V, so this is
about the expected size.

Open: the red channel does not cooperate. The three belt stars, nearly one
colour, give R/G of 0.44, 0.06 and 0.03, so it is not measuring the star; for
Saiph it comes out at zero, and Betelgeuse's R/G is no higher than
Rigel's, which is the wrong way round. Suspect the red sky background (sodium
and LED glow) or the sensor's red response; needs more than one frame.
